## Substack Notes（5本）

**Note 1 — 業種の答え**
Which Japanese sector has the highest share of companies with no named successor?
Construction, at 57.3% (Teikoku Databank, 2025).
But that's the wrong question to stop at. Many of those owners close instead of selling. This week I read three datasets side by side.
[LINK]

**Note 2 — 黒字廃業**
52.8% of the Japanese companies that closed in 2025 were profitable in their last reported year.
Average owner age: 74.9.
These weren't failing businesses. Their owners stopped.

**Note 3 — 金融機関の切り口**
A surprising cut in Japan's succession data: who the company banks with.
• Megabank clients: 44.2% have no successor
• Local co-operative bank (shinkin) clients: 57.9%
The least-planned owners sit with the lenders foreign buyers rarely talk to.

**Note 4 — 地域差**
Japan's successor gap by prefecture ranges from 33.9% (Mie) to 73.7% (Akita).
Akita is the only prefecture above 70% — and the only one where the rate has risen three years running.

**Note 5 — 内部昇格**
For the first time, more Japanese companies handed the top job to an insider (36.1%) than to family (32.3%) in 2025 (preliminary).
Good news for buyers, with a caveat: open to an insider isn't the same as open to an outsider.

## LinkedIn（2本）

**LinkedIn 1**
If you're searching for a small business to buy in Japan, your first decision isn't the company. It's the sector.

I put three Japanese datasets side by side for this week's The Japan Handover:
– Who has no successor (Teikoku Databank, ~270,000 companies)
– Who is closing down (Tokyo Shoko Research, 67,210 closures in 2025)
– Who is failing for lack of a successor (461 bankruptcies in the year to March 2026)

Construction shows pressure on every measure. Services has the most exits of every kind. Manufacturing has the smallest gap — partly because it got the most support, which likely means more competition for deals.

The full sector table, plus cuts by company size, region and lender, is in this week's issue. Link in comments.

**LinkedIn 2**
One practical point for anyone looking at Japanese construction companies:

A construction company's operating license stays with the company if you buy its shares. If you buy only the business or assets, the license doesn't move automatically — since October 2020, it can be carried over only with prior approval.

That one structuring choice can decide whether the company can keep bidding for work the day after closing.

More on how to read Japan's sector data as a buyer in this week's The Japan Handover. Link in comments.
